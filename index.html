Two manuscripts on seeing people from the air

Both studies address aerial computer vision under difficult visual conditions. Results are reported separately for each dataset, and neither manuscript has been published.

VGMKG Manuscript in preparation First Author

Visibility-Guided Multimodal Transformer with Knowledge Graph for Aerial Pedestrian Attribute Recognition

Authors
𝐄Ibtissam Toure, Hussein Samma, Abubaker Salih
Affiliation

This work treats the visibility of body parts as an explicit signal when recognizing pedestrian attributes in aerial imagery. It combines visual, textual, and structural representations, and uses a heterogeneous knowledge graph to reason over relationships between body parts and attribute groups.

Research problem

Recognizing pedestrian attributes in aerial imagery is challenging because of limited visibility, occlusion, small pedestrian appearance, and incomplete visual information. This research explores how explicit visibility information and relationships between body parts can improve attribute recognition.

Key contributions

  1. Visibility-aware reasoning: body-part visibility is used as an explicit signal during prediction.
  2. Multimodal architecture: visual, textual, and structural representations are integrated.
  3. Knowledge graph reasoning: relationships between body parts and attribute groups are modeled directly.
  4. Ablation analysis: regional and graph pathways improved Mean Accuracy by 2.13 percentage points over the visual-only baseline. Text added another 0.92 points when the graph was present.

Methodology

  • DINOv2 ViT-L/14Global and regional visual representations
  • OWLv2 + SegFormer-B2Visibility estimation across 17 body parts
  • Sentence-BERTEncoding of visibility descriptions
  • Knowledge graphHeterogeneous graph with 26 nodes
  • Graph TransformerTwo layers for relational reasoning
  • Fusion TransformerMultimodal fusion for final attribute prediction

Limitations

  • The main dataset is synthetic.
  • Additional real-world aerial validation is needed.
  • Rare classes and visibility estimation errors remain challenges.

Geometry-Aware Multimodal Transformer for Human Action Recognition in Crowded Aerial Scenes

Authors, in manuscript order

This work combines skeletal geometry with visual features to recognize human actions in crowded aerial scenes. Geometry-guided attention aligns pose-derived information with localized image patches, and a Transformer fuses the modalities for the final prediction.

Research problem

Human action recognition in crowded aerial scenes is challenging because of overlapping bodies, visual clutter, pedestrian scale variation, and occlusion. This research investigates how geometric and visual information can be combined to improve recognition in these scenes.

Key contributions

  1. Geometry-guided attention: cross-modal attention aligns geometric representations with localized visual patch features.
  2. Skeletal geometry representation: pose keypoints are expressed as normalized distances from the pedestrian center.
  3. Transformer-based fusion: modalities are combined using a learnable fusion token.

Methodology

  • DINOv2 ViT-L/14Frozen visual backbone with a lightweight residual adapter
  • DETRPedestrian detections
  • ViTPosePose estimation
  • Skeletal geometryNormalized distances of pose keypoints from the pedestrian center
  • Viewpoint embeddingsLearnable embeddings, evaluated without improving results (see limitations)
  • Cross-modal attentionGeometry-guided alignment with localized visual patch features
  • Fusion TransformerMultimodal fusion using a learnable fusion token

Ablation study: removing geometry

Effect of removing the geometry component on the synthetic crowded-scene dataset.
Mean Accuracy
Full model 85.6%
Without geometry 82.9%
Macro-F1
Full model 77.0%
Without geometry 72.3%

Limitations and future work

  • The viewpoint modality did not provide an improvement, partly because of distribution imbalance and its derivation from the same keypoints.
  • The main results come from a synthetic dataset, so evaluation on larger real-world crowded-scene datasets is needed.
  • Viewpoint imbalance requires further investigation.

Munsit

Real-time Arabic accessibility system

An AI system for Deaf and hard-of-hearing users in Arabic-speaking environments. Three modules form one pipeline: Arabic speech recognition with Whisper, Arabic sign-letter recognition from MediaPipe hand landmarks, and environmental sound classification with a CNN. Results appear in a Streamlit interface, and recognized signs are spoken through text-to-speech.
